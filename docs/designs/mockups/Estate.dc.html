When one of you dies

The plan assumes the first death at the earlier "plan to age" on the Household tab: Alex at 94 in 2057.

Default 70%: a single person needs about 71% of a couple's spending for the same standard of living.

What the plan does at the first death

  • RRSP, RRIF, LIF, TFSA and non-registered accounts pass to the survivor without tax.
  • The deceased's OAS stops. Their CPP becomes a survivor benefit, capped at the CPP maximum.
  • Workplace pensions continue at their survivor share (set on the Savings tab).
  • The survivor files as a single person, so tax often rises.

What you leave behind

Measured after the second death, after all taxes and probate, in today's dollars.

Leave at 0 if you have no target.

Counts toward the legacy and probate, pays no capital gains tax, grows with inflation and is never sold to pay for spending.

Ontario probate: $15 per $1,000 of estate value above $50,000.Edit
RRSP, RRIF, LIF and TFSA are assumed to name a beneficiary, so they skip probate. Non-registered accounts and the home go through the estate.
On the final return, whatever is left in RRSP, RRIF and LIF is taxed as income, and non-registered investments are taxed as if sold.
